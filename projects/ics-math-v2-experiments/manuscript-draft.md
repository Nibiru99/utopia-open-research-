# ICS-Math v2: Controlled Experimental Validation of a Symbolic Equation Manifold

**Preprint manuscript draft v0.1**
**Author:** David Glowalla
**Status:** draft assembled from inbox-original experiment packages; not yet released

## Abstract

We report eight controlled toy-simulation experiments validating Infinity Counting Sphere Math v2 (ICS-Math v2), a geometric representation layer for mathematical symbols, equations, uncertainty states, and transformation structures. Using the B3 working layout (pi-arc coordinate placement plus mirror-pair graph metadata), the sequence covers symbol placement, pi-arc family separation, equation encoding, equation transformation, ambiguity-state classification, base-10 ghost-core benchmarking, fragment-loop retrieval, geometric imprint caching, and complexity-scaling robustness. The experiments support architectural validity of the symbolic manifold under declared controls. They do not claim replacement of conventional arithmetic, general mathematical reasoning competence, or physical discovery.

## 1. Position in the research trail

ICS began as a recursive information-packing sphere. ICSv2 upgraded it into a symbolic equation manifold. This deposit publishes the first consolidated experimental validation of that upgrade. Later layers (ICSv3 observer/compute, ICSv4 value dynamics, ICSv5 mutual proprioception) and the GEO instrument lane are out of scope except as cited lineage.

## 2. Methods summary

Each experiment ships documentation, metrics tables/JSON, and SHA-oriented summaries where present in the original packages. The consolidated report evidence/ICS-Math_v2_Experimental_Results.md is the narrative spine. Readers should treat per-experiment folders as the immutable evidence units.

## 3. Results summary

The consolidated report states that Experiments 1A–8 completed the controlled validation sequence with passing results under the declared toy-simulation protocol. Specific numeric outcomes remain those recorded in the per-experiment metric files; this manuscript does not invent aggregates beyond that report.
